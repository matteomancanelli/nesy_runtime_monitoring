\section{Theoretical Comparison}
\label{sec:theory-cmp}

Having presented the three paradigms individually, we now compare them along the dimensions that matter for a monitor: what each can represent, and where each breaks down.

\subsection{Three Achilles Heels}
\label{sec:theory-heels}

Each paradigm is exact and efficient on a large class of specifications, and each has a single structural weakness that is dual to the others', so that no one paradigm dominates.
%
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Symbolic DFA --- state-space blow-up.} The \ltlf-to-DFA translation is doubly exponential in the worst case, so the compiled automaton (and hence the compile-time cost and memory) can explode even when the runtime visits only a handful of states.
    \item \textbf{RuleRunner --- temporal-instance conflation.} The one-register-per-subformula state can merge evaluations of the same subformula originating at different temporal offsets, so it returns wrong verdicts when that merged register is not a sufficient semantic summary (Section~\ref{sec:rulerunner-limitation}).  The exact safe class is decidable per formula, bounded-future instances admit a fixed pipeline repair (Section~\ref{sec:rulerunner-bounded}), and full \ltlf completeness is recovered through the residual vocabulary of progression (Section~\ref{sec:rulerunner-progression}).
    \item \textbf{DeepDFA --- explicit transition cost.} The transition tensor is indexed by the full alphabet $2^{|\calP|}$. A factored backend can replace that unconditional tensor by disjoint guard cubes, which is often compact but remains exponential in the worst case, and every realization retains the $|Q|^2$ matrix update (Section~\ref{sec:deepdfa-alphabet}). The representations themselves and their measured scaling are the subject of a companion paper \paperB{}.
\end{itemize}
%
Two clarifications keep this comparison honest. First, the state-space blow-up is not exclusive to the symbolic paradigm: DeepDFA is compiled from the \emph{same} minimal DFA, so it inherits $|Q|$ (its tensor is $|Q|^2 \cdot 2^{|\calP|}$) and suffers the same doubly-exponential worst case. We list state blow-up under the symbolic monitor because it is that monitor's \emph{only} structural weakness, whereas for DeepDFA the \emph{additional}, representation-specific cost that the symbolic monitor escapes is the alphabet axis. Second, the symbolic and automata-based heels are duals: a DFA transition function is a table with $|Q| \cdot 2^{|\calP|}$ entries, and each paradigm compresses a different axis of it, paying on the axis it leaves explicit. The symbolic monitor compresses the symbol axis with Boolean guards and keeps an explicit state, so its cost driver is $|Q|$ and it can never suffer an alphabet blow-up; dense DeepDFA compresses the state axis into a uniform batchable matrix product and therefore needs one matrix per symbol. Neither weakness can be swapped onto the other paradigm without discarding the representational choice that defines it. We use this duality only to place the three heels; the representation design space it opens is developed in a companion paper \paperB{}.

One further symmetry is worth stating explicitly, because it is easy to present as an advantage of one paradigm. Exact \emph{three-valued timing} --- announcing \textsc{satisfy} or \textsc{violate} at the first prefix after which the verdict is permanent --- is a property of the continuation language, not of any local state. Every paradigm therefore pays for it with a global reachability analysis: the symbolic monitor and DeepDFA precompute traps and accepting sinks on the compiled DFA, the eager progression monitor classifies its reachable residual graph, and the bounded-event monitor classifies its reachable composite states. Each of these is a fixed label head, computed once and read out per cell; none of them drives the recurrence. What distinguishes the paradigms is the size of the graph that must be classified, not whether such a graph is needed at all, and a monitor that skips the analysis pays in latency rather than in soundness.

As monitors of crisp traces, the completeness-restored RuleRunner and fixed DeepDFA are semantically interchangeable: the progression construction and the minimal DFA recognize the same \ltlf language (Section~\ref{sec:rulerunner-progression}), although the reachable residual graph need not itself be minimal. The reason to keep both lineages is what each could expose beyond crisp monitoring: a future learned DeepDFA would parameterize transitions between opaque automaton states, while a future rule-based learner could attach parameters to syntactically-indexed rules. These are two hypotheses for the gradient-based specification adaptation deferred to future work (Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}), not capabilities evaluated here.
