# Bounded-event RuleRunner: verified semantics and exact eligibility

For the concise status of the original, bounded-event, and progression
implementations together, see [`rulerunner_status.md`](rulerunner_status.md).
This note is the authoritative detailed specification of the bounded version;
it makes no benchmark or performance-result claim.

This note replaces the speculative parts of `bounded_horizon_RR.md`.  It keeps
the useful bounded-future insight, but separates three questions that the
handoff conflated:

1. When is the **original** RuleRunner correct?
2. Which subformulas have a statically finite future horizon?
3. When can finite-horizon subformulas be evaluated by a pipeline and safely
   supplied as events to the original RuleRunner?

The executable definitions are in
[`src/monitors/rulerunner/equivalence.py`](../src/monitors/rulerunner/equivalence.py)
and [`src/monitors/rulerunner/bounded.py`](../src/monitors/rulerunner/bounded.py).
Their two neural realizations are in
[`src/monitors/rulerunner/bounded_cilp.py`](../src/monitors/rulerunner/bounded_cilp.py).

## 1. The exact RuleRunner correctness class is formal and decidable

For an LTLf formula $\varphi$, let $RR_\varphi$ be the deterministic finite-state
monitor induced by the published RuleRunner rules and let $A_\varphi$ be the
canonical DFA.  Define

\[
  \mathcal C_{RR}
  = \{\varphi \mid L(RR_\varphi)=L(A_\varphi)\}.
\]

This is an exact maximal *semantic* class, not an empirical approximation.
Membership is decidable: explore the synchronous product of the reachable
RuleRunner carry states and DFA states over every valuation of the formula's
atoms.  A reachable pair with different final acceptance gives a
counterexample.  Breadth-first exploration gives a shortest one.

The implementation also distinguishes:

- **language equivalence:** final verdicts agree after every finite trace;
- **prefix soundness:** every definite early RuleRunner verdict agrees with the
  DFA's exact permanent label;
- **online-label equivalence:** RuleRunner and the DFA issue the same label at
  every prefix, including cases where RuleRunner is merely later.

An exact maximal grammar is a different and much less natural object.  LTLf
identities make unsafe-looking syntax harmless (`F(F a)`), while sharing an
equal subformula between temporal offsets can make simple-looking syntax unsafe.
There is no unique preferred grammar presentation of the semantic class.
Accordingly, the paper uses the Boolean closure of temporal clauses with
propositional operands as a proved sufficient flat fragment;
`certify_rule_runner` remains the exact per-formula decision procedure outside
it.

### Corrected smallest examples

`X(X a)` is **not** a counterexample.  Its two Next nodes have different keys,
and the inner node is activated once, after the outer node advances.  Exact
checking certifies `X^k a` for $k=1,\ldots,6$; exhaustive traces through length 9
were also checked during development.

A small genuine alias is

\[
  (X a) \land X(X a).
\]

The syntactically equal `X a` occurrence is reached at two different offsets but
is represented by one deduplicated RuleRunner register.  The two-cell trace
$(\emptyset,\{a\})$ is a shortest witness: RuleRunner satisfies and the DFA
violates.

The operational source of the defect is therefore:

> One RuleRunner register simultaneously denotes evaluations originating at
> different trace positions, and OR-merging their values is not a sufficient
> semantic summary.

Reinstallation by `F/G/U/R` is the most common source, but it is not the
definition of the defect.  Conversely, a collision can be harmless through
operator-specific absorption or idempotence.

### Certificates are an offline artifact

Certification is exact but expensive: it compiles the canonical DFA with MONA
and explores the synchronous product over $2^{|\mathcal P|}$.  Running it inside
`compile()` would put a DFA construction into the compile-time cost of the
construction that claims to avoid one, so any compile-cost measurement would be
biased against the bounded monitor by its own safety check.

`src/monitors/rulerunner/certificates.py` therefore stores the results offline
in `certificates.json`, regenerated by

```bash
python -m src.monitors.rulerunner.certificates --refresh
```

Each record pins an MD5 fingerprint of the rule system it certifies.  Rebuilding
that rule system is cheap -- no MONA, no product exploration -- so the
fingerprint is checked on every lookup, and a certificate that no longer matches
the current `rules.py` is treated as absent rather than trusted.  The monitors
take a `certificate` mode: `"cached"` (artifact only -- what evaluation must
use, so no hidden DFA construction is possible), `"auto"` (default; certify in
memory on a miss without modifying the artifact), or `"recompute"`.
`monitor.certificate_source` records
which path was taken.

With `certificate="cached"` and the default `exact_online=False`, compiling a
bounded-event monitor touches no automaton at all.
For either online mode, the shared timing harness enforces the
cached-certificate policy and aborts on a miss; it cannot silently fall back to
certification.

## 2. Syntactically bounded event formulas

For the current, unparameterized LTLf syntax, define the transparent bounded
grammar

\[
\begin{aligned}
  \beta ::= {}& p \mid \neg\beta
  \mid \beta\land\beta \mid \beta\lor\beta \mid \beta\to\beta \\
  &\mid X\beta \mid WX\beta.
\end{aligned}
\]

Its horizon is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 h(p)&=0, & h(\neg\beta)&=h(\beta),\\
 h(\beta_1\circ\beta_2)&=\max(h(\beta_1),h(\beta_2)),
 &h(X\beta)=h(WX\beta)&=1+h(\beta).
\end{aligned}
\]

Standard `F/G/U/R` are classified as unbounded.  Semantic simplification can
show special formulas containing them to be bounded, but that is intentionally
separate from this auditable syntactic test.  Finkbeiner--Kuhtz's
interval-indexed Until is not added as surface syntax: every fixed finite bound
can be unrolled into the Boolean and Next fragment above, so native support
would improve only input succinctness and is outside this project's scope.

Let $\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_m$ be the maximal non-atomic bounded islands of
$\varphi$.  Replace each distinct island with a fresh event proposition $e_i$,
obtaining the **RuleRunner skeleton** $\alpha$.

Examples:

| Original formula | Bounded event | Skeleton |
|---|---|---|
| $G(Xa)$ | $e\equiv Xa$ | $Ge$ |
| $F(a\land Xb)$ | $e\equiv a\land Xb$ | $Fe$ |
| $G(a\to Xb)$ | $e\equiv a\to Xb$ | $Ge$ |
| $a\ U\ (b\land Xc)$ | $e\equiv b\land Xc$ | $a\ U\ e$ |
| $G(a\to Fb)$ | none | unchanged |

The last row is not repaired: the inner `F b` has no finite horizon, and exact
checking rejects the unchanged skeleton.

## 3. Event-pipeline semantics

Let

\[
  H=\max_i h(\beta_i).
\]

For a non-empty finite trace $w=\sigma_0\cdots\sigma_{n-1}$, define a derived
word $D_\varphi(w)$ of the same length.  Its cell at position $t$ retains every
original atom needed by $\alpha$ and satisfies

\[
 e_i\in D_\varphi(w)_t
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 w,t\models\beta_i.
\]

Because $\beta_i$ has horizon $h(\beta_i)$, this value is fixed once position
$t+h(\beta_i)$ has arrived.  A streaming transducer therefore keeps at most
$H+1$ observations:

1. append the new observation to the window;
2. once the window contains $H+1$ cells, evaluate all islands at its oldest
   position and emit one derived cell;
3. remove that oldest observation;
4. when end-of-trace is declared, evaluate and emit the remaining suffix
   positions, using strong-Next=false and weak-Next=true beyond the boundary.

The empty word has no position at which an event atom can be emitted.  Its value
is therefore stored explicitly as $\epsilon(\varphi)$, computed from LTLf empty-
trace semantics.  This matters when the complete formula is itself a bounded
island, for example `WX a`.

### Composition theorem

**Theorem (bounded-event composition).**  Let $\alpha$ be the eventized
skeleton of $\varphi$.  If $\alpha\in\mathcal C_{RR}$, then the bounded-event
pipeline followed by $RR_\alpha$ returns the correct final LTLf verdict for
every finite trace, using $\epsilon(\varphi)$ on the empty trace.

**Proof sketch.**  For every non-empty $w$, structural induction on $\varphi$
after replacing the maximal islands gives the substitution lemma

\[
  w,0\models\varphi
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  D_\varphi(w),0\models\alpha.
\]

The pipeline emits exactly $D_\varphi(w)$ in chronological order: positions at
least $H$ behind the input are mature, and the final flush evaluates the finite
suffix explicitly.  Since $RR_\alpha$ is language-equivalent to its DFA, its
final verdict on that derived word is correct.  The empty trace follows from the
separate boundary value. $\square$

If the skeleton RuleRunner emits a permanent verdict before the flush, it is
sound for the original trace as well: ignoring the not-yet-mature derived cells
only admits more continuations, and a verdict permanent under all derived-word
continuations is permanent under the realizable subset.

**The converse does not hold, and the delay is not bounded by $H$.**  An earlier
version of this note claimed a delay of at most $H$ cells, reasoning that the
derived prefix trails the input by at most $H$.  That is true of the *prefix*
but not of the *verdict*.  Eventization discards the constraint that only some
derived words are realizable -- in particular that the last position of any
trace falsifies every strong-Next event -- so the skeleton's DFA may consider
continuations that no original trace produces, and can therefore fail to see a
permanence that already holds.

The witness is `G(X a)`, which is unsatisfiable on every non-empty trace: its
last position has no successor, so `X a` is false there.  The canonical DFA
traps after one cell.  Its skeleton is `G e`, and a derived word with `e` true
everywhere is perfectly consistent with `G e`, so the skeleton stays
`UNDECIDED` for the whole trace and only reports `VIOLATE` at the flush.  The
lag is the trace length, not $H$.  This case is pinned by
`test_skeleton_latency_is_not_bounded_by_the_horizon`.

So the two configurations differ in guarantee, not only in cost:

| configuration | final verdict | online labels | compile-time cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| default (`exact_online=False`) | exact | sound, **arbitrarily late** | eventization + certificate lookup only |
| `exact_online=True` | exact | exactly the DFA's | reachable composite graph over $2^{|\mathcal P|}$ |

### Exact three-valued extrapolation

Requesting `exact_online=True` removes the delay for the monitor API.  Its live state
is the tuple consisting of the delayed RuleRunner carry, its last evaluated
cell, the observation window and its current fill level.  This tuple induces a
finite deterministic machine over the original observations.  For each
reachable tuple, flushing the window determines whether the prefix itself is
accepted.  Reverse reachability from accepting and rejecting tuples then gives
the exact accepting sinks and traps:

- a tuple is an accepting sink iff no rejecting tuple is reachable;
- it is a trap iff no accepting tuple is reachable;
- otherwise its label is `UNDECIDED`.

The resulting state cubes are compiled into a fixed CILP readout: one hidden
unit per reachable composite state, whose body fixes every feature to a
polarity.  That is a compiled state classifier rather than a compositional
circuit, and it should be described as such.  This head does not drive
recurrence; the shift register, event evaluator, and certified RuleRunner
skeleton remain the recurrent construction.

**It is not free, and on the natural family it is not even cheap.**  The
composite state includes the window contents, so the graph grows roughly as
$2^{|\mathcal P| \cdot H}$ times the skeleton carry.  Measured on the
bounded-response family, the head is already larger than the DFA it avoids
constructing:

| formula | $H$ | composite states | DFA $|Q|$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| `G(a -> X b)` | 1 | 11 | 3 |
| `G(a -> (b \| Xb \| X^2 b))` | 2 | 41 | 4 |
| `G(a -> (b \| Xb \| X^2 b \| X^3 b))` | 3 | 157 | 5 |
| `G(a -> (b \| Xb \| X^2 b \| X^3 b \| X^4 b))` | 4 | 613 | 6 |

This is why the head is opt-in.  The construction whose cost claim is
interesting -- a fixed static pipeline with no residual closure and no
automaton -- is the default configuration; exact online timing is a priced
add-on, and any evaluation must report the two separately.  Compilation is
guarded by `DEFAULT_MAX_STATES` and raises `ExtrapolationLimitExceeded` rather
than exploring indefinitely.  Because the composite
state recognizes exactly the continuation language of the original prefix, its
three labels are identical to the canonical DFA's labels for the original
formula.

This is an adaptation of the Finkbeiner--Kuhtz extrapolation principle to the
project's LTL3-style contract.  Their native output is truth under strong/weak
truncated-path semantics, whereas `step()` here returns a definite verdict only
when it is permanent under every finite continuation.  The reverse-reachability
classification is what bridges that semantic difference.

## 4. Relation to Finkbeiner--Kuhtz

Borrowed ideas:

- recursive finite temporal horizons;
- maximal bounded separation formulas;
- a fixed observation pipeline;
- evaluation of bounded future over the buffered suffix;
- composition with a monitor for the remaining unbounded prefix formula.

Important differences:

- their logic includes a compact parameterized bounded-Until syntax, which is
  expressible here by finite Boolean/Next expansion;
- they use strong/weak truncated-path contexts and an extrapolation circuit;
- their prefix component is built through alternating/universal automata;
- our prefix component is specifically the original RuleRunner and is admitted
  only after exact equivalence certification;
- our extrapolation readout targets permanent three-valued LTLf labels, rather
  than their strong/weak truncated-prefix Boolean output.

The bounded pipeline itself is prior art.  The potentially publishable point is
its use as a verified middle repair for RuleRunner's register aliasing, together
with the exact skeleton eligibility test and the comparison with full
progression.

## 5. Implementation and verification status

Implemented now:

- exact BFS product certifier with shortest witnesses;
- separate language, prefix-soundness, and online-equivalence results;
- bounded-horizon analysis and maximal-island eventization;
- fixed-window streaming pipeline and finite-suffix flush;
- a recurrent CILP shift register containing the last $H$ observations (the
  current cell supplies the $(H+1)$-st input directly);
- two-valued CILP event circuits, compiled separately for every finite suffix
  length $1,\ldots,H+1$ so strong and weak Next receive the correct boundary;
- a flat realization that pools the unrolled rules and iterates them to their
  fixpoint;
- a structured realization with one addressable module per
  `(subformula, offset)`, executed in dependency postorder;
- public flat and structured monitor wrappers that require exact language and
  prefix-soundness certificates for the old-RuleRunner skeleton;
- exact three-valued extrapolation by reachable composite-state classification,
  compiled into a fixed CILP readout;
- fused batch evaluation: equal-length suffix windows share event-circuit
  tensor passes, after which all derived traces use the existing batched flat or
  structured skeleton runner;
- exhaustive composition tests for `G X a`, `F(a & X b)`, `G(a -> X b)`,
  `a U (b & X c)`, `F(a & X X b)`, and the direct shared-register
  counterexample `(X a) & X(X a)`.

The public implementations are `BoundedEventRuleRunnerMonitor` and
`BoundedEventStructuredRuleRunnerMonitor`.  Both use the same eventization,
latency, suffix-flush, and certification semantics.  They differ only in the
grouping of the event rules and in whether the certified skeleton uses the flat
or structured old-RuleRunner CILP realization.

The paper also defines a readable sufficient class for this construction:
eventize the maximal bounded islands and require the resulting skeleton to be
in the original RuleRunner's proved flat fragment.  This class strictly
contains the flat fragment (for example, it adds `F(a & X b)`), while exact
skeleton certification admits additional identity-dependent cases.

Exact extrapolation has an additional compile-time price: the current
implementation explicitly explores the reachable composite graph over
$2^{|\mathcal P|}$ observations.  The compiler exposes atom/state guards and
fails explicitly rather than silently approximating.  The fused batch path is
now suitable for measurement, but no benchmark result is claimed until the
deferred evaluation phase is rerun.
