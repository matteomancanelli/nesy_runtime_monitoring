\section{The Two Axes of a Transition Table}
\label{sec:duality}

A DFA transition function is a table with $|Q| \cdot 2^{|\calP|}$ entries, and each
representation compresses a different axis of it to obtain its advantage, paying on the
axis it leaves explicit. The symbolic monitor compresses the $2^\calP$ axis with symbolic
guards --- keeping an explicit current state, so its cost driver is $|Q|$ --- and this is
exactly why it cannot suffer an alphabet blow-up: it never enumerates symbols. Dense
DeepDFA compresses the state axis into a uniform, differentiable, batchable matrix
product --- which requires one explicit transition matrix per symbol, so its cost driver
is $2^{|\calP|}$. Neither weakness can be swapped onto the other representation without
discarding the very representational choice that defines it. The factored representation
(Section~\ref{sec:deepdfa-factored}) attempts to compress \emph{both} axes at once, and
recovers flat-in-$|\calP|$ scaling whenever the guards admit compact disjoint covers.
Read-once structure is one sufficient case, not a necessary boundary.

State blow-up is not exclusive to the symbolic paradigm: DeepDFA is compiled from the
\emph{same} minimal DFA, so it inherits $|Q|$ (its tensor is $|Q|^2 \cdot 2^{|\calP|}$)
and suffers the same doubly-exponential worst case. The \emph{additional},
representation-specific cost that the symbolic monitor escapes is the alphabet axis.
